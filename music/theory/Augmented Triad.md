#### Visualizing Common Examples

To turn a standard major triad into an augmented triad, you simply raise the top note (the 5th) by one half-step:

- **C Augmented (Caug / C+):** C – E – G♯ _(raised from C–E–G)_
- **G Augmented (Gaug / G+):** G – B – D♯ _(raised from G–B–D)_
- **F Augmented (Faug / F+):** F – A – C♯ _(raised from F–A–C)_ 

#### Why It Sounds "Floating"

Unlike major and minor triads, the augmented triad has no perfect fifth. The perfect fifth is what gives chords their sense of stability and grounding. Without it, the augmented triad sounds open, unresolved, whole-tone, and mysterious. It creates musical tension that strongly wants to pull (resolve) to a more stable chord. 

#### How It Is Used in Music

- **Creating Suspense:** Film composers use it to signal dream sequences, magic, or mystery.
- **The "Lead-In" Chord:** It is frequently used as a passing chord to smoothly connect a major chord to another chord (e.g., C → Caug → Am).
- **Pop Culture Hook:** You can hear it as the very first opening chord of **"Oh! Darling" by The Beatles** or in the intro theme of **The Simpsons**.
